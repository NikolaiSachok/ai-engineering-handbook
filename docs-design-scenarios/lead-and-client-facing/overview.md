# Lead & client-facing

A track for the decisions that have to be explained as well as made. Elsewhere in this course the question is
what a system should do. Here it is what a lead is actually asked: in what order, ruling out what, on whose
authority — and then whether the answer survives being repeated by somebody who was not in the room.

Scenarios here share a shape. The engineering is not the hard part; the engineering is settled in a paragraph
and then has to be chosen between. What separates answers is the ordering — which property of the situation
gets tested first, what that test eliminates before the next one runs — and whether the elimination was
computed from the numbers on the table or merely asserted with conviction. The second half is a translation
problem: the same decision put to a commercial director, a regulator, or a client who has heard a simpler
story from a competitor, without the argument turning into either a lecture or a promise.

## In this track

- **[Where the knowledge lives](/design-scenarios/lead-and-client-facing/where-the-knowledge-lives)** — a
  400-million-token technical corpus, part of it under per-customer NDA, and five months to the conference;
  which criterion you apply first, what it rules out, and how you put that to the person who signs it off.

One scenario so far. The track opens with it because the archetype is already well attested across the
postings this course samples — the roles that ask for architecture ownership almost always ask for
stakeholder communication in the same paragraph — but the sampling that fills a track runs per scenario, and
nothing else here is written yet. When a second one lands it will appear above this line, not in a plan.
